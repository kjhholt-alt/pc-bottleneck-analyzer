## 1. Enable XMP/EXPO in BIOS — Cost: $0 | Impact: 10–20% FPS

This is the single most common performance issue we see in system scans, and it costs absolutely nothing to fix.

When you buy DDR5-6000 RAM, it doesn't run at 6000 MT/s out of the box. It runs at the JEDEC default — DDR5-4800 — unless you manually enable the XMP (Intel) or EXPO (AMD) profile in your BIOS. That means your expensive memory is performing like budget RAM.

The impact is larger than you'd think. In CPU-bound scenarios (competitive shooters, RTS games, anything running at 1080p), enabling XMP/EXPO delivers:

- **10–15% higher average FPS** in most games
- **15–25% better 1% low frames** — the metric that determines how smooth a game feels
- **Measurably faster asset loading** in open-world titles

### How to Enable XMP/EXPO

1. Restart your PC and enter BIOS (usually Del or F2 during boot)
2. Look for "XMP," "EXPO," or "Memory Profile" — it's typically on the main page or under an overclocking tab
3. Select Profile 1 (there's almost never a reason to choose anything else)
4. Save and exit

That's it. If your system is unstable after enabling XMP, your motherboard may need a BIOS update — check the manufacturer's website for the latest version.

## 2. Upgrade to Dual-Channel RAM — Cost: $25–$45 | Impact: 20–40% FPS

If you're running a single stick of RAM, this is your biggest bottleneck and the highest-impact upgrade on this list.

Single-channel RAM cuts your memory bandwidth in half. Modern CPUs — especially AMD Ryzen — are extremely sensitive to memory bandwidth. In CPU-bound games, the difference between single-channel and dual-channel is staggering:

These aren't theoretical numbers — they reflect real-world testing across hundreds of system configurations.

If you're still running games off a mechanical hard drive in 2026, this is the upgrade that will feel like you bought a new computer.

An HDD isn't just slow for loading screens. Modern games stream assets continuously while you play — textures, models, audio, world data. When the game needs data faster than your HDD can deliver it, you get stutters, pop-in, and frame drops that have nothing to do with your CPU or GPU.

A SATA SSD solves this completely:

- **Load times:** 70–80% faster. A 45-second HDD load becomes 8–12 seconds.
- **In-game stutters:** Asset-streaming stutters disappear entirely.
- **Texture pop-in:** Massively reduced or eliminated in open-world games.
- **OS responsiveness:** If your Windows drive is still an HDD, moving to an SSD transforms everyday usability.

## 4. Upgrade Your CPU Cooler — Cost: $25–$45 | Impact: 5–15% FPS If Throttling

Stock CPU coolers — the ones that come in the box with Intel and AMD processors — are designed to keep the chip alive, not to keep it fast. Most stock coolers allow the CPU to hit thermal limits under sustained load, which triggers thermal throttling: the CPU reduces its clock speed to avoid overheating.

If your CPU hits 95–100°C while gaming, it's throttling. That means it's not running at the boost clocks it's capable of, and you're losing FPS that a $30 cooler would give back.

### How to Check If You're Throttling

Open [HWiNFO64](https://www.hwinfo.com/) or use our [PC Bottleneck Analyzer](https://pcbottleneck.buildkit.store) scanner, then play a demanding game for 10 minutes. Check:

- **CPU temperature:** If it's sustained above 90°C, you're likely throttling.
- **Clock speeds:** If the CPU isn't holding its rated boost clock (check your CPU's specs), thermal throttling is the cause.
- **Thermal throttling flag:** HWiNFO64 has a specific "thermal throttling" sensor — if it ever reads "Yes," you need a better cooler.

## 5. Add Case Fans — Cost: $15–$30 | Impact: 3–8% FPS + Better Stability

Many pre-built PCs and budget builds ship with just one or two case fans — usually a single rear exhaust. This creates a negative-pressure environment where hot air has no efficient path out of the case, and your GPU and CPU run hotter than they need to.

Adding intake fans to the front of your case is one of the cheapest upgrades with a surprisingly meaningful impact:

- **GPU temperatures drop 5–10°C**, which means higher sustained boost clocks
- **CPU temperatures drop 3–7°C**, reducing or eliminating throttling
- **More consistent frame times** because components maintain stable clock speeds

### What to Buy

- **Best value:** <AffiliateLink name="Arctic P12 PST 5-pack" /> (~$25 for five 120mm fans). These are incredibly effective for their price and the PST daisy-chain connector makes installation simple.
- **Budget single fan:** <AffiliateLink name="Noctua NF-P12 redux" /> (~$14) if you just need one quality fan.

### Optimal Fan Configuration

For most cases, the ideal setup is:

- **2 front intake fans** (pulling cool air in)
- **1 rear exhaust fan** (pushing hot air out)
- **Optional:** 1 top-rear exhaust fan

This creates positive air pressure, which means air is pushed out through gaps in the case instead of being sucked in — reducing dust buildup and keeping components cooler.

## 6. Replace Thermal Paste — Cost: $8–$12 | Impact: 5–15°C Temperature Drop

Thermal paste degrades over time. After 2–3 years, the thermal interface between your CPU and its cooler becomes less effective, leading to gradually increasing temperatures. If your PC used to run cool but now runs hot under the same workloads, dried-out thermal paste is likely the culprit.

### How to Do It

1. Remove your CPU cooler (usually 4 screws)
2. Clean the old paste off the CPU and cooler base with isopropyl alcohol (90%+) and a coffee filter or lint-free cloth
3. Apply a pea-sized dot of new thermal paste to the center of the CPU
4. Reinstall the cooler — the mounting pressure spreads the paste evenly

The whole process takes 15 minutes and requires no special skills.

## 7. Free Software Optimizations — Cost: $0 | Impact: 5–15% FPS

Before spending any money, these software fixes can recover significant performance that's being silently wasted:

### Windows Power Plan

Switch to **High Performance** or **Ultimate Performance** power plan. The default "Balanced" plan aggressively downclocks your CPU to save power, which can cost 5–10% FPS in CPU-bound games.

**How:** Search "Power Plan" in Windows → Select "High Performance." For Ultimate Performance (hidden by default), open Command Prompt as admin and run:
```
powercfg -duplicatescheme e9a42b02-d5df-448d-aa00-03f14749eb61
```

### Update GPU Drivers

Outdated GPU drivers are one of the most common causes of poor performance in new games. Both NVIDIA and AMD regularly release "game ready" drivers that can improve FPS by 5–15% in specific titles.

- **NVIDIA:** Download from [GeForce Experience](https://www.nvidia.com/geforce-experience/) or the NVIDIA website
- **AMD:** Download from [AMD Software: Adrenalin Edition](https://www.amd.com/en/technologies/software)

### Disable Startup Programs

Open Task Manager (Ctrl+Shift+Esc) → Startup tab → Disable everything you don't need running at boot. Common FPS thieves:

- Discord (runs in background — launch manually when needed)
- Spotify desktop app
- OneDrive sync
- Adobe Creative Cloud
- Hardware RGB software (Corsair iCUE, NZXT CAM, Razer Synapse)

Each unnecessary startup program consumes RAM and CPU cycles. Disabling 5–6 of them can free up 1–2GB of RAM and 5–10% of CPU capacity.

### Enable Game Mode

Windows Game Mode (Settings → Gaming → Game Mode) tells Windows to prioritize your game's process and prevents Windows Update from installing updates or restarting during gameplay. It's a small but free improvement.

## What NOT to Spend Money On

Some "upgrades" that PC forums love to recommend are a waste of money at this budget level:

- **RGB RAM kits for "faster" speeds.** If you already have dual-channel DDR4-3200 or DDR5-5600, going to DDR4-3600 or DDR5-6000 gains maybe 2–3% FPS. Not worth $60+.
- **NVMe SSD when you already have a SATA SSD.** The difference in gaming is 1–3 seconds per load screen. Save that money for a real component upgrade.
- **"Gaming" Ethernet cables or USB drives.** Marketing nonsense. A $5 Cat6 cable performs identically to a $50 "gaming" cable.
- **Windows debloating scripts from random forums.** These risk system instability and rarely deliver measurable FPS gains beyond what the manual optimizations above provide.
