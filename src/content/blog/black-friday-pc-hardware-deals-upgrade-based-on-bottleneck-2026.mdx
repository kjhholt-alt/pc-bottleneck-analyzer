## Step 1: Identify Your Bottleneck Before You Shop

The single biggest mistake gamers make on Black Friday is buying hardware that looks like a good deal without checking whether it addresses their system's weakest link. A 40% discount on a component you do not need is not a deal — it is waste.

Here is a quick self-diagnosis if you have not run our scanner yet:

### Signs of a CPU Bottleneck
- GPU usage stays below 90% while gaming
- FPS does not improve when you lower resolution or graphics settings
- CPU usage hits 90-100% on multiple cores during gameplay
- Stuttering in CPU-heavy games like Civilization VII, Cities: Skylines 2, or large Minecraft worlds

### Signs of a GPU Bottleneck
- GPU usage pegged at 95-100% while gaming
- FPS improves significantly when you lower resolution or turn down settings
- CPU usage stays comfortably below 70%
- Visual quality settings are the main lever for performance

### Signs of a RAM Bottleneck
- Task Manager shows memory usage above 85% while gaming
- Stuttering when alt-tabbing or switching between game and browser
- Running DDR4 at 2133-2400 MHz or single-channel configuration
- Only 8 GB or 16 GB with heavy background apps

### Signs of a Storage Bottleneck
- Long load times (60+ seconds in open-world games)
- Texture pop-in during fast movement
- Hitching when entering new areas
- Still running a mechanical hard drive as your game drive

Once you know your bottleneck, skip to that section below.

### Storage Shopping Tips
1. **2 TB is the sweet spot.** Modern games are 80-150 GB each. A 1 TB drive fills up fast; 2 TB gives you room to keep 15-20 games installed.
2. **Gen4 is enough for gaming.** Gen5 NVMe drives are faster on paper but cost 2-3x more and deliver zero additional FPS. Save the money for other components.
3. **Check your M.2 slot generation.** If your motherboard only has PCIe 3.0 M.2 slots, a Gen4 drive will still work — it just runs at Gen3 speeds (~3,500 MB/s instead of 7,000+). Still a massive upgrade over a hard drive.
4. **Clone, do not reinstall.** Use free cloning software like Macrium Reflect or Samsung Data Migration to move your OS and games to the new drive without reinstalling everything.

## Black Friday Traps to Avoid

Not every deal is a good deal. Watch out for these common Black Friday traps:

1. **"Was $599, now $399!" on last-gen GPUs.** An RTX 4070 at $399 looks like a steal until you realize a faster RTX 5070 is $499. Check the generation before buying.
2. **DDR4 kits marked as "gaming RAM" at premium prices.** DDR4-3200 32 GB kits should cost $45-55 at most. Do not pay $80+ for RGB or a "gaming" label.
3. **QLC NVMe drives disguised as deals.** Some cheap SSDs use QLC NAND that slows down dramatically when the SLC cache fills. Stick with the brands and models listed above.
4. **PSU bundles with unknown brands.** A cheap PSU can damage every component in your system. Stick with trusted brands: Corsair, Seasonic, EVGA, be quiet!, or Thermaltake at the GX tier and above.
5. **Pre-built PCs with one good component and everything else cheap.** A system with an RTX 5070 but a Celeron CPU and 8 GB of RAM is not a gaming PC. Always check the full spec sheet.

## Timing Your Purchases

Black Friday is not actually one day anymore. Here is the typical deal timeline for 2026:

- **Nov 18-24 (this week):** Early deals from Amazon, Newegg, and Best Buy. Some of the best prices appear here and do not come back on Black Friday itself.
- **Nov 25-26 (Thanksgiving week):** More deals leak. Most retailers match competitors' early prices.
- **Nov 27 (Black Friday):** Doorbuster deals — limited quantity, first-come-first-served. Best for pre-built PCs and monitors.
- **Nov 28-Dec 2 (Cyber Monday week):** Often the best time for components. Retailers push remaining inventory with deeper discounts.
